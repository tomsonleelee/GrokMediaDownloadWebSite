Download Grok videos in HD

Ask Grok for HD versions of your videos in one run, then download the highest quality available for each one.

Add to Chrome See PRO pricing

Updated November 28, 2026 · written for extension version 2.6.3

Short answer

Grok videos can exist in three qualities: SD, HD (720p) and 1080p. With GrokMediaDownloader PRO, open ✨ HD Upgrade in the popup, choose 🌐 Whole Library or 🌟 Favorite, click 🚀 Upgrade Quality and confirm the per-tier preview. Each video takes about 8 seconds. 1080p is opt-in and needs a SuperGrok Heavy subscription on your Grok account. Afterwards, 1. Generate List and 2. Start Download save the highest quality available for every video.

SD, HD-720p and 1080p in Grok

The extension sorts every video into one of three tiers. An upgrade moves a video up one tier at a time.

SD

The standard-definition file. A video counts as SD until Grok has a higher-resolution version of it.

HD / 720p

A 720p version. Videos get one through an HD upgrade. Videos that Grok generated at 720p already count as HD and are not upgraded again.

1080p

Full HD, the highest tier. Upgrading to 1080p requires a SuperGrok Heavy subscription on your Grok account; without it, Grok refuses the request.

Before you start

  • GrokMediaDownloader 2.6.3 or newer. Chrome updates extensions automatically; to force it, open chrome://extensions, turn on Developer mode and click Update.
  • A PRO license. HD Upgrade and video downloads are PRO features, a one-time $4.99 purchase (pricing). Paste your GMD-… key into 🔑 License in the popup; these keys need extension 2.6.0 or newer.
  • Grok open in the same Chrome profile, on grok.com/library or any other grok.com page.
  • For 1080p only: a SuperGrok Heavy subscription on that Grok account.

If you don't have PRO yet, the last section of this guide shows what the free version covers. You can install the extension from the Chrome Web Store and try it with images first.

Step by step

  1. 1

    Step 1: Open the HD Upgrade section

    Click the extension icon on your grok.com tab and expand ✨ HD Upgrade. Without PRO, the section shows a 🔒 Pro Feature lock.

  2. 2

    Step 2: Choose what to scan

    Under 🎯 Upgrade Source, keep 🌐 Whole Library (the default) or pick 🌟 Favorite. Since Grok's July 2026 change, newly generated videos are missing from Favorites for many accounts, so a Favorite scan can call videos "already upgraded" when it simply cannot see them.

    A whole-library scan checks the quality of every video and can take a long time on a large library. Tick Only upgrade videos from the last … hours and enter a number from 1 to 240 to limit it to recent videos.

  3. 3

    Step 3: Decide on 1080p

    Upgrade HD/720p to 1080p is off by default. Tick it only if your Grok account has SuperGrok Heavy, as the note under the box says. With the box off, the extension sends no 1080p requests at all.

    If Grok refuses 1080p during a run, the extension stops asking for 1080p for the rest of that run, keeps doing the HD upgrades and tells you how many videos it skipped.

    Upgrades go one step at a time: SD to HD, and HD/720p to 1080p. An SD video therefore needs two runs to reach 1080p. The first run makes it HD, and a second run with the 1080p box ticked makes it 1080p.

  4. 4

    Step 4: Scan and check the preview

    Click 🚀 Upgrade Quality. On the whole library the button counts up ⚙️ Resolving video quality while each video is checked.

    A 📊 Scan Result dialog then lists the total number of videos, how many are SD, HD/720p and 1080p, and how many will be upgraded now. Click OK to start or Cancel to stop. If every video is already at its highest available quality, the extension says so and sends nothing. In the example below the 1080p box is off, so the last line says how many videos could still go to 1080p.

  5. 5

    Step 5: Let the upgrade run

    Each video takes about 8 seconds, so 100 videos need around 13 minutes. While it runs, the button reads ⏹ Upgrading (x/y), Stop?, and clicking it cancels the run.

    Keep the grok.com tab open until the run ends. If you close the popup, open it again to see the progress. At the end the popup reports ✅ Upgrade Complete! Upgraded: x/y.

  6. 6

    Step 6: Generate the download list

    Grok finishes upgrades on its own servers, so a video can show its old quality for a short while after the request was accepted. Give it a few minutes. Then pick the same source under 📥 Download Source & Filter, make sure 🎬 Videos (Pro) is ticked and click 1. Generate List.

    When the scan ends, a 📊 Download Summary dialog lists the videos to download by quality (1080p, HD, 720p and SD) and asks "Start download?". OK starts right away; Cancel leaves 2. Start Download enabled for later. The status panel then counts SD, 720p, HD and 1080p videos separately, each with its total, what is already downloaded and what still needs downloading.

  7. 7

    Step 7: Download the highest quality

    Leave ✨ HD Priority on in ⚙️ Settings; it is on by default. The extension then saves one file per video and picks 1080p first, then HD, then the original. With HD Priority off it saves the original and the HD version side by side.

    If you clicked Cancel in the summary, click 2. Start Download. A progress bar shows the estimated time left, and ⏹ Stop Download and ▶ Continue Download pause and resume the run. Videos are saved under grok_downloads/ in Chrome's Downloads folder.

Timing and rate limits

Grok limits how many requests an account can make in a short time. The extension is built to slow down rather than fail:

  • HD Upgrade sends one request per video and takes about 8 seconds per video. Plan around 13 minutes for every 100 videos.
  • If Grok answers that there are too many requests, the extension waits a few extra seconds, counts that video as failed and continues. Run the upgrade again later. Videos that were upgraded drop out of the list, so only the rest are sent.
  • In a whole-library scan, videos whose quality could not be checked because of rate limiting are skipped rather than guessed. The Scan Result dialog tells you how many.
  • During downloads, the extension pauses after every 250 videos to stay within Grok's limits, for 3 minutes by default (adjustable in ⚙️ Settings), and then continues on its own. The Download Summary warns you when a run has more than 250 videos.
  • On very long runs Grok may start refusing downloads, for example with HTTP 403 after thousands of videos. Reload the grok.com tab and click 2. Start Download again; the download history skips everything already saved.

Upgrade first, then download

The order matters for files you have already downloaded. A video saved in SD before its upgrade is downloaded again as HD on the next run, next to the SD file.

HD and 1080p share one download record per video. A video you already downloaded at 720p is not downloaded again after a 1080p upgrade. Run the 1080p upgrade before downloading, or clear that source's history in 🧹 Clear Actions to download everything from the source again. Clearing a history deletes nothing on Grok or on disk, and a file that already exists is saved with a number added to its name instead of being overwritten.

Troubleshooting

What usually goes wrong with HD upgrades and HD downloads, and what to do about it.

HD Upgrade says all videos are already at the highest quality
Check 🎯 Upgrade Source. If it says Favorite, switch to 🌐 Whole Library, because the Favorite list can miss videos generated after Grok's July 2026 change. If the 1080p box is off and your videos are HD, the message also tells you how many of them could still go to 1080p.
"1080p upgrade requires a paid plan — skipped N video(s)"
Grok refused 1080p for your account, which happens without a SuperGrok Heavy subscription. Untick Upgrade HD/720p to 1080p. HD (720p) upgrades are not affected.
The scan takes very long
On 🌐 Whole Library the extension checks every video before it can show the preview. Tick Only upgrade videos from the last … hours to limit the scan, and widen the window in a later run.
After the upgrade, Generate List still shows SD
Give Grok a few minutes to finish, then click 1. Generate List again. If some requests failed because of rate limits, run 🚀 Upgrade Quality again; only videos that are still below their target are sent.
I upgraded to 1080p, but Start skips the video
The video was already downloaded at 720p, and HD and 1080p share one download record. Clear that source's history in 🧹 Clear Actions and start again. Next time, run the 1080p upgrade before the first download.
The Upgrade Quality button or the Videos checkbox is locked
Both are PRO features. Activate your key in 🔑 License (format GMD-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX, extension 2.6.0 or newer), or see pricing.

Frequently asked questions

What resolution is HD for Grok videos?

HD means 720p. The extension treats 1080p as a separate, higher tier. A video that Grok generated at 720p already counts as HD and is not upgraded to HD again.

Do I need SuperGrok Heavy to download Grok videos in HD?

Only for 1080p. The HD (720p) upgrade does not ask for it. Upgrading to 1080p requires a SuperGrok Heavy subscription on your Grok account; without it, Grok refuses the request and the extension skips those videos.

How long does HD Upgrade take?

About 8 seconds per video, so roughly 13 minutes for 100 videos. A whole-library scan adds time before the upgrade starts, because the quality of each video is checked first.

Is HD Upgrade free?

No. HD Upgrade and video downloads are part of PRO, a one-time $4.99 purchase with lifetime updates. The free version downloads images. See the pricing page for everything PRO includes.

Does upgrading delete or replace the original video?

The extension only asks Grok to create a higher-resolution version and never deletes anything. With ✨ HD Priority off, you can download the original and the HD version side by side.

Can I upgrade only my newest videos?

Yes. In ✨ HD Upgrade, tick "Only upgrade videos from the last … hours" and enter 1 to 240 hours. Videos outside that window are left as they are.
